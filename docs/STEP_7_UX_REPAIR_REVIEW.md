# Step 7 — first-encounter repair pass

Implemented for review, 22 September 2026. This is the UX implementation pass, not a claim that real child research or deployment is complete.

## Experience

The opening view now points to the current world's story and distinguishes it from loading a new experiment. The camera starts at the spark. Following the story activates the matching top-level destination; its ending offers “Change the ending” and a route into civilisation collapse. The guidance display sits above the map, where a laptop user can see feedback while changing a control.

The world has a faint geographic/contour texture, scattered slow light activity and a slow atmospheric sweep. This is decorative context, not a geographic prediction. Model connections remain stable: all declared edges exist before selection; secondary connections are subdued, and selecting a component illuminates its neighbourhood. Harm and selected-route signals move along the actual causal wires.

Motion defaults to ON, including in the Codex browser that reports reduced motion, as explicitly requested. An on-map Motion control can pause all continuous animation. There is no forced sound or rapid flashing. Verified computed signal-flow animations and changing dash offset in the live browser; verified the pause state stops animation.

## Range and incentives

The main physics and probability rules were not inflated to create drama. A moderate monthly “One warning, many endings” experiment complements the extreme stress worlds. Zero-count outcomes offer investigation rather than a disabled control. Feedback explains warning timing, fixed human choices, absent approval, sufficient hospital backup and production bottlenecks. Beneficial AI repair assistance is now discoverable among safeguards.
